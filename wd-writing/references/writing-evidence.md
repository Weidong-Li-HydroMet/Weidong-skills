# Pedram writing: Public evidence and actionable writing rules

Research date: 2026-10-18. This document synthesizes public work; it is not a set of writing instructions confirmed by Pedram himself. See `sources.json` for source records and `paper-patterns.md` for each paper's structure and reading coverage. Prioritize reasoning and judgment rather than imitating personality or mechanically substituting favorite phrases.

Deduplicate preprint versions and the published article within the same source family. Weights help select references; they do not make factual claims more or less true.

## Rules that can be applied directly in the skill

The confidence levels below refer to how consistently these writing practices appear in public samples. They are not probability estimates of the advisor's future reactions.

| ID | Editing action | Evidence and location | Confidence / limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| PW01 | First state the scientific question in one sentence. List established progress, unresolved tensions, and tests that distinguish explanations before drafting the introduction. | [W01](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25835v1) §1; [W05](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgad015) Introduction; [W06](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-17130-7) Introduction. | High; there is no requirement to manufacture a contradiction. |
| PW02 | Check the abstract for six functions: background/gap, question, test, main result, mechanism or interpretation, and scope and significance. Functions may be combined; six sentences are not required. | W01 Abstract; [W03](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12130898/) Abstract; [W02](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20365v3) Abstract. | High; the PRL abstract begins directly with the finding, illustrating why neither sentence count nor background length should be fixed. |
| PW03 | A paper title may state a question, mechanism, or concrete contribution, depending on the genre. Do not impose the slides' rule of conclusion-based titles on every paper. | W01 and W03 have question titles; W04 juxtaposes promises and challenges; W05 concerns an explanatory mechanism. | High; these are observed titles, not an advisor-prescribed format. |
| PW04 | Organize the introduction's literature around research approaches or competing explanations. Each paragraph should explain why existing work cannot answer the current question; end by defining the scope of the paper's answer. | W04 §1; W05 Introduction; W01 §1. | High; neither a fixed number of paragraphs nor maximizing citation count is required. |
| PW05 | Order Results by how the evidence builds: establish the phenomenon → key controls → mechanism diagnostics → limits/generalization. End each section by explaining what the next step needs to rule out. | W01 §2.1–2.4; W03 Results; W05 results-to-framework sequence. | High; theoretical papers may derive first and validate afterward, as in W13. |
| PW06 | Write tests and interpretations separately: use verbs of different strength for direct observations, mechanistic interpretations, and untested hypotheses. Writing cannot supply evidence for missing experiments. | W01 §2.3–2.4 and Discussion; W04 §3.3; W03 Summary and Discussion. | High; do not weaken every clear finding with “may” merely to sound cautious. |
| PW07 | When reporting improvements or failures, specify the target, variable, region, lead time, baseline, data split, and uncertainty. In particular, check whether different methods are being compared on the same task. | W03 Fig.1–2; W06 Discussion; W02 Fig.2–3. | High; add only conditions that materially affect interpretation, avoiding a main text overloaded with configuration details. |
| PW08 | State what each metric can and cannot answer. Treat global means, offline correlations, online stability, extreme tails, and physical consistency separately. | W04 §3.3–4; W03 Summary points; W05 Fig.2. | High; do not upgrade one metric into a claim of across-the-board superiority. |
| PW09 | Explain in the main text the key controls needed to rule out alternative explanations. Even when a figure or detailed table is in the SI, the main text must state which step in the reasoning it supports. | W03 matched-size control / SI S1–S3 references; W01 alternative training-data explanations; W06 bias-correction references. | High; this does not require all control figures to appear in the main text. |
| PW10 | Make captions self-contained: include the aim of the comparison, panel correspondence, variables and units, samples/ensembles, meanings of lines and shading, processing or normalization, and necessary cross-references. | W01 Fig.1–4; W03 Fig.2, 5; W05 Fig.2; W06 Fig.2, 4. | High; caption length follows information needs. Do not apply the slides' rule of extremely short text. |
| PW11 | Audit the statistical unit underlying every shaded band, error bar, and mean. IQR, standard deviation, standard error, and variation across events/models/initial conditions are not interchangeable. | W03 Fig.2/5; W05 Fig.2; W02 Fig.1/2. | High; verify specific statistical definitions in the original caption rather than inferring them from appearance. |
| PW12 | Keep the chain of reasoning and limitations that could change the conclusions in the main text. Use Methods/SI for solvers, extended derivations, complete sensitivity analyses, and additional variables/configurations. | W01 Appendix A/B; W04 Appendices A–F; W05 Methods and SI pointers; W06 Methods and Supplementary references. | High; journal layouts differ, so do not prescribe a universal section location. |
| PW13 | In the Discussion, answer the research question first, then address competing explanations, links to prior work, limitations, and the next testable investigation. Avoid merely repeating results. | W01 §3; W03 Summary and Discussion; W06 Discussion. | High; derive practical implications along a chain supported by demonstrated findings. |
| PW14 | Distinguish explicitly between transferability of a particular conclusion and transferability of an analytical method. Define the system/model scope before discussing broader applications. | W05 Discussion; W06 Discussion; W02 concluding paragraphs. | High; do not describe a method's potential as if it had already been validated in a new system. |
| PW15 | When discussing inconsistent findings, first align the regions, timescales, measured quantities, and experimental designs before deciding whether the findings conflict. Preserve unresolved disagreements. | W06 Discussion. | Moderately high; this is a strong example from a single older paper and also a general scientific practice. |
| PW16 | Revise evidence and logic first, then paragraphs, then sentences. Finally, check that claim strength is consistent across the title, abstract, results, and discussion. | An operational workflow synthesized from PW01–PW15. | **Suggested workflow**; not an advisor's instruction explicitly stated in the source material. |

## Concrete outputs in practice

1. For a complex argument or full manuscript task, consider first providing a claim map. For a local request, check it internally without requiring extra output: `question → claim → supporting figure/table → key control → limitation → appropriate location`. Mark nonexistent data as missing evidence; do not fill in numbers.
2. For paragraph-by-paragraph edits, use `original sentence/paragraph → proposed revision → reason for change → evidence/logic/expression label`. For a small task, revise only the relevant portion.
3. Check scientific support, structure, sentences, and captions separately in the completed draft. A reviewing agent must consult the user's data and primary literature directly, not treat this profile as a source of scientific facts.
4. When imitation is requested, describe the output as “suggestions based on public writing samples,” not “Pedram would definitely require this.” State explicitly that the user's instructions, journal requirements, and current evidence take priority over the profile's defaults.

## Original editing examples (not quotations or established results)

- Overly broad: `Our model captures the physics.` → Fill in only when evidence is available: `Under [tested regime], the model reproduces [diagnostic] relative to [reference]; whether it captures [untested process] remains unresolved.` Basis for the change: PW06–08.
- Performance alone: `Method A is better than B.` → `At [lead time], A reduces [metric] by [value with uncertainty] on [independent test]; the advantage does not extend to [tested exception].` Delete the final clause if no exception was tested; do not invent a limitation.
- A sequence of results: `We next plot X.` → `To distinguish [explanation 1] from [explanation 2], we test [controlled comparison].` Use this only if the experiment can actually distinguish those explanations.

## What must not become fixed rules

Do not treat the latest research conclusions as permanent truths; require every paragraph to use Here/However; imitate preprint typesetting errors; infer the lead writer from author order; attribute all coauthors' wording to PH; infer Methods/SI structure from an abstract; automatically reuse a paper's data or claims in the user's project; or treat positive evaluations, reputation, or citation counts as evidence of style. Limit section-level attribution according to explicit contribution statements.
