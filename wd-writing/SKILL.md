## Starting a task

First determine whether the user wants help with the argument, structural planning, a local rewrite, or a full manuscript review. Read the manuscript and necessary figures and tables, retaining the target journal, audience, length, and existing notation. Use the user's preferred language for discussion and retain the manuscript's language unless the user requests a translation.

First assess whether a claim is supported, then refine how it is expressed. Identify where data, statistical tests, citations, or necessary definitions are missing; never fill an evidentiary gap with stronger wording. Preserve the user's numerical values, units, experimental conditions, and the identity of the methods used. Explicitly explain the reasons for any substantive change to a conclusion.

## Argument and revision

- **Framing the problem:** Tell readers early which phenomenon needs explaining, what is missing from current explanations or methods, and how the paper distinguishes competing explanations. Do not declare scientific novelty merely because the study uses a new model.
- **Abstract:** Organize the background/gap, key tests, most important findings, mechanism or interpretation, boundaries, and significance according to the evidence and the journal's space constraints. Include only actual contributions; avoid fixed sentence counts and unsupported sweeping conclusions.
- **Introduction:** Move from a concrete problem to a contradiction or unknown, then explain what evidence is needed and what the paper supplies. Make the literature serve the argument rather than listing papers author by author. Use a counterintuitive opening only when the material supports it.
- **Results:** Make each section answer a question, with the preceding result naturally motivating the next test. Do not mechanically follow the order in which experiments were performed. Whether the model hierarchy moves from complex to simple or simple to complex, it must serve the specific reasoning.
- **Mechanisms:** Distinguish findings, interpretations, and alternative explanations. State what the controlled experiments rule out and which factors remain unidentified; correlation or high predictive skill alone does not establish a mechanism.
- **Figures and captions:** Explain the figure's role in the argument, then provide the available information about panels, comparison targets, definitions, units, samples, and uncertainty. Do not invent error bars, statistical significance, or experimental settings.
- **Discussion:** Return to the question posed in the introduction, synthesize the findings, and distinguish direct results, transferable interpretations, and broader implications. Clearly identify the conditions under which the conclusions hold and the unresolved questions. Divide content between the main text and SI according to what readers need to understand and verify the argument; do not shorten the paper by hiding limitations.

These are adaptable practices inferred from the samples. When applying a specific rule, consider its source and genre; do not impose PRL's space constraints or the rhetoric of a single article on every manuscript.

## Delivering revisions

For a local request, provide a usable rewrite directly, with reasons for the key changes. For a full manuscript task, first provide a diagnosis of the central argument and a proposed structure, then deliver the revision. Do not force a lengthy review report on a user who only wants the finished text.

Clearly distinguish wording changes, reorganization of the argument, and suggestions that require new evidence. Do not conceal changes in scientific meaning within language editing. Cite public writing samples only when needed to explain a source; do not transplant their results or citations into the user's manuscript as the user's own evidence.

When useful, offer a few formulations with different levels of claim strength and explain which is supported by the available evidence. Avoid treating resemblance to Pedram's writing as the sole criterion: accuracy, clarity, and alignment with the paper's purpose determine whether a revision succeeds.

When the user provides actual edits from their advisor, preserve the mapping between the original text, revised text, and reasons, distinguished by date and project. Incorporate these into the persistent skill only after an explicit request to update it. Generated imitations cannot serve as evidence of actual writing.
