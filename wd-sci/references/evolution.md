This is a sharper and more specific argument than a broad emphasis on the potential benefits of physics-informed approaches. ## Updating and Handling Conflicts

1. **Prioritize content dates over publication labels.** Record submission/revision dates, the version actually read, and whether the final version has been examined. Relevant main-text sections of S09's 2026-02-02 v3 have now also been read; S17 still uses the inspected 2025 v2. Neither final text has been verified in full, so publication labels alone must not update their content dates.
2. **Weight personal attribution and scientific credibility separately.** S02's explicit writing contribution makes it better suited to inferring current public judgment; its preprint status still limits the strength of scientific conclusions. A jointly authored review does not automatically become a personal position.
3. **First test whether a conflict is real.** Align tasks, variables, scales, lead times, data distributions, and evaluation metrics. Opposite conclusions under different conditions may be entirely reasonable.
4. **Keep a change record when new material changes a rule.** State the old rule, its supporting evidence, the new evidence, changed conditions, and uncertainty. Do not simply accumulate the newest abstracts or delete historical qualifications.
5. **Do not use public material to fill in unknown private preferences.** Specific group-meeting feedback, reviewing habits, or experiences communicating with students that were not verified in this collection remain unknown. They may later be calibrated using explicit feedback from him provided by the user.
